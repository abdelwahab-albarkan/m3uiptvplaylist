## What Is IPTV: The short version

Traditional cable sends every channel down a coax line all the time, and your box tunes into one. IPTV works more like a website: when you pick a channel, your device asks a server for that stream, and the server sends it to you over your internet connection. Nothing comes in that you haven't asked for, which is why IPTV needs a steady connection but no cable line or dish.

## The parts of an IPTV setup

### What Is IPTV: 1. The service

The IPTV service runs servers that deliver live channels, and often on-demand movies and series. When you subscribe, you get login details.

### 2. The login: M3U or Xtream Codes

- **M3U playlist:** one long link that points to a text file listing every channel and its stream address.
- **Xtream Codes API:** a server address, username and password. The player uses them to request channels, movies, series and the guide in an organized way.

Our [M3U and Xtream Codes guide](/guides/m3u-playlist) explains both in more depth.

### 3. The player app

The player is the app on your TV or phone, such as TiviMate, IPTV Smarters Pro or IBO Player. It reads your login, shows the channel list and plays the video. The player doesn't include channels; it's like a web browser for TV streams. See [our player comparison](/apps).

### 4. The TV guide (EPG)

The Electronic Program Guide is a separate data feed that tells the player what's on each channel and when. It's what turns a long list of streams into a cable-style grid. Here's [how EPG works](/guides/iptv-epg).

### 5. Your home network

Video arrives as a steady flow of small packets. HD needs around 8 to 10 Mbps per screen and 4K around 25 Mbps. Just as important is *consistency*: a connection that dips for even a second causes buffering. That's why Ethernet or strong 5 GHz Wi-Fi matters so much.

## What about creating your own channels?

Hotels, venues and campuses can distribute their own video over a network. They use a device called an encoder, which turns a video source into an IP stream. Our [IPTV encoder guide](/guides/iptv-encoder) explains how that works.

## Features you'll hear about

- **Catch-up / time-shift:** replay programs from the past few days, if the service supports it for that channel.
- **VOD:** video on demand, meaning movies and series you can start any time.
- **Multi-screen / connections:** how many devices can stream at the same moment on one plan.
- **Recording:** some players can record to the device's storage.

## IPTV vs cable vs streaming apps

| | Cable / satellite | Streaming apps (Netflix, etc.) | IPTV via player app |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Hardware | Rented box | App on your device | App on your device |
| Live channels | Yes | Mostly no | Yes |
| Guide grid | Yes | No | Yes, with EPG |

## Is IPTV legal?

The technology is completely legal and used by major companies. What matters is whether a particular service holds the rights to the channels it offers.
