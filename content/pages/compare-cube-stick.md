## Fire Cube vs Fire Stick: The short answer

### Essential recommendations for Fire Cube vs Fire Stick

- **Choose a Fire TV Stick** if you just want to stream, have a limited budget, or want a device hidden behind the TV.
- **Choose a Fire TV Cube** if you want hands-free Alexa, built-in Ethernet, or the extra power for a main home theater TV.

## Side-by-side comparison

These details are based on the 3rd-generation Cube and the Fire TV Stick 4K Plus and 4K Max. Check Amazon's current pages, because specs can change.

| | Fire TV Cube (3rd gen) | Fire TV Stick 4K Plus / 4K Max |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form | Small box on a shelf | Stick behind the TV |
| Hands-free Alexa | Yes | No; press the remote button |
| Ethernet | Built in | Needs an adapter |
| HDMI input | Yes, for a cable box or console | No |
| USB port | Yes | No standard port |
| Storage | 16 GB | 8 GB or 16 GB |
| Price tier | Higher | Lower |

## Where the Cube is better

**Performance.** The Cube has a faster processor than the sticks. Apps open quicker, and it has more headroom for big channel guides.

**A wired connection.** The built-in Ethernet port is useful if your Wi-Fi struggles, especially for live sports and 4K. You don't need any separate adapter. Our [buffering guide](/troubleshooting/iptv-buffering) explains why a wired connection helps.

**Hands-free control.** You can say "Alexa, turn on the TV" without touching a remote, and the Cube can control compatible soundbars and cable boxes as well.

**Extra ports.** The HDMI input lets you pass a device such as a cable box through the Cube, and the USB port can be used for accessories that the Cube supports.

## Where the stick is better

**Price.** A stick costs much less. You get the same major streaming apps, and the 4K Plus and 4K Max support Dolby Vision.

**Tidiness.** A stick plugs straight into the TV and stays out of sight, with no shelf space needed.

**Simple setup.** There's no separate HDMI cable, placement to plan, or microphone to manage.

**Travel.** You can pack a stick for a hotel or a vacation home, assuming the TV and network allow it.

## Does the Cube give better picture quality?

For most viewers, no. Both can deliver 4K with HDR, and the source and your internet connection have a much larger effect. The Cube does include a feature that upscales lower-resolution video, but the difference is subtle on many TVs and shows.

## Privacy and always-on microphones

Hands-free use means a microphone is always listening for the wake word. The Cube has a microphone-off button, which also turns off hands-free control. Review the Alexa privacy settings in your account if you're concerned about how voice recordings are handled. If you don't want a hands-free microphone at all, a stick is the simpler choice.

## How to decide

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **Will you actually use hands-free Alexa?** If not, a big part of the Cube's value goes away.
2. **Do you need Ethernet?** If you do, compare the cost of a stick plus Amazon's adapter with the price of the Cube.
3. **Is this for your main TV?** The Cube makes more sense for a TV you use every day than for a guest room.
