## Where cable still wins

- **One bill and one remote** for everything, with a provider who comes out to fix problems.
- **A managed network,** so the TV picture doesn't compete with your home Wi-Fi.
- **Full regional sports coverage** in some markets that streaming services don't carry.

If those matter most to you, it may be worth calling your provider to negotiate a lower rate before switching.

## Where the alternatives win

- **No box fees or surcharges.** Cable bills often grow through equipment rental, broadcast TV fees and regional sports fees.
- **No contracts.** Streaming services and most IPTV plans run month to month.
- **Use the devices you own,** from Fire TV to smart TVs to phones.
- **Mix and match:** add a sports service during football season and drop it in spring.

## Building a cable replacement

Most successful cord-cutters combine two or three pieces:

1. **Local channels:** an antenna, or a live TV service that carries your locals. See [local channels without cable](/guides/iptv-local-channels).
2. **Live cable networks and sports:** a licensed live TV service ([here's how to compare them](/compare/live-tv-streaming-services)) or an IPTV service. If you consider IPTV, use our [provider checklist](/guides/how-to-choose-iptv-provider) and read [the legal side of IPTV](/guides/is-iptv-legal-usa) first.
3. **On-demand:** the streaming apps you already pay for, plus free services for background TV.

## Free live TV streaming

Free ad-supported services are better than many people expect ([see the full guide](/guides/free-live-tv-streaming)): Pluto TV, Tubi, The Roku Channel, Samsung TV Plus and Amazon's free channels together offer hundreds of live-style channels, including news, classic shows and movies. They won't replace live sports, but they're an easy way to cut costs.

## Equipment you'll need

- A streaming device for each TV, or smart TVs with the right apps. Not sure which? Read [Roku vs Fire Stick](/compare/roku-vs-fire-stick).
- A solid home network: Ethernet or 5 GHz Wi-Fi to the main TV.
- Optionally, an antenna and a network tuner for local channels on every screen.

## Bottom line

Cable is the simplest choice and usually the most expensive. A streaming service plus an antenna is the safest full replacement.
