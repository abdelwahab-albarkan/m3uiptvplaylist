## Google TV vs Fire TV: The quick answer

### Essential recommendations for Google TV vs Fire TV

- **Choose Google TV** if you use Android or Google services, want Google Cast, or want broad app choice in the main store.
- **Choose Fire TV** if you use Alexa or Prime Video heavily, or find a Fire OS stick at a good sale price.

For IPTV players, a **Fire OS** stick and a **Google TV** device are both good choices. Newer **Vega OS** Fire TV Sticks are the exception, so read our [Fire TV Stick comparison](/compare/firestick-models) before buying one.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Google TV | Fire TV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home screen | Recommendations from your services | Amazon content and promotions up front |
| App store | Google Play | Amazon Appstore |
| Sideloading | Available on most devices | Fire OS only, not Vega OS |
| Voice assistant | Google Assistant or Gemini, depending on device | Alexa |
| Casting | Google Cast built in | No Google Cast |
| Smart home | Google Home | Alexa and Ring |
| Hardware | Many brands | Mostly Amazon sticks and partner TVs |

Features vary by device and change with software updates.

## Home screen and ads

Neither platform is free of promotion. Google TV leans on recommendations from across your subscribed services, plus some sponsored content. Fire TV tends to give more space to Amazon's own content and advertising. Google TV may feel more neutral if you don't use Prime Video much, but this is partly personal taste, so try each one on a friend's TV or in a store if you can.

## Apps

Mainstream services are available on both. For anything less common, it's worth checking:

1. **Search the store on the exact device** or the store's website.
2. **Check the software version.** A Vega OS stick has a smaller catalog than Fire OS.
3. **Look at app ratings for that platform.** The same app can work well on one and poorly on the other.

Both Fire OS and Google TV can run popular IPTV players, including [TiviMate](/apps/tivimate). Setup is covered in our [Fire TV](/setup/firestick) and [Google TV](/setup/android-tv) guides.

## Voice and smart home

Your existing smart home is often the tie-breaker. Alexa on Fire TV works well if you have Echo speakers or Ring cameras, and you can view compatible cameras on your TV. Google TV fits better with Nest devices and Google Home. If you don't use either, choose based on the other factors here.

## Casting from your phone

Google TV has a clear advantage if you like to send video from your phone. Most Android apps and many iPhone apps have a Cast button that simply works. On Fire TV, casting support is more limited and depends on the app.

## Hardware choice

Google TV runs on a wide range of devices, from budget boxes to high-end TVs, which gives you more choice but also more variation in quality. Fire TV hardware is mostly Amazon's own sticks and Cube, plus TVs from partner brands. That makes buying simpler but gives you fewer options, particularly if you want a built-in Ethernet port.

## Buying tips for either platform

- **Don't choose on discount alone.** A cheap stick with too little memory will frustrate you every day.
- **Check for 4K and HDR** if you have a suitable TV. See [what real 4K needs](/guides/4k-8k-iptv).
- **Consider Ethernet** if your Wi-Fi is weak or the TV is far from your router.
- **Check update support** on any older device you're offered at a low price.

## Our recommendation

If you're already invested in Alexa, stay with Fire TV and choose a Fire OS model for the widest app choice. Otherwise, Google TV is a strong default, especially if you cast from your phone. Either way, both can replace a built-in TV system, so your choice isn't permanent. If you're an Apple household, see [Apple TV vs Fire Stick](/compare/apple-tv-vs-fire-stick) as well.
