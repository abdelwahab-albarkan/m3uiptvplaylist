## Google TV Streamer vs Chromecast: Key differences

### Essential recommendations for Google TV Streamer vs Chromecast

| | Google TV Streamer | Chromecast with Google TV (4K) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Status | Current model | Discontinued |
| Memory | 4 GB | 2 GB |
| Storage | 32 GB | 8 GB |
| Ethernet | Built in | Needs a USB-C adapter or hub |
| Form | Small box on a shelf | Dongle behind the TV |
| Remote | Included, with a find-my-remote feature | Included |
| Smart home | Built-in support for Google Home, including Matter and Thread | Fewer smart home features |

Prices vary and specs can change, so confirm them on Google's store before you buy.

## What you'd actually notice

**Fewer storage warnings.** This is the most obvious everyday change. The Chromecast's 8 GB fills up quickly once you install several apps and updates. With 32 GB, you can keep far more apps without having to manage storage.

**Smoother multitasking.** Double the memory helps when you switch between apps and when you use large apps, like IPTV players with a long channel guide. Don't expect a dramatic difference in raw processor speed, though. The Streamer isn't a high-end streaming box.

**A more stable connection.** The built-in Ethernet port removes the need for a separate hub. This is a major benefit if your TV is far from the router or your apartment has congested Wi-Fi.

**A different physical setup.** The Streamer sits on a shelf rather than hanging behind the TV. That's a benefit for airflow and for the Ethernet cable, but a drawback if you want a completely hidden device.

## Who should upgrade

Upgrade if you often see these problems on your Chromecast:

- Regular "low storage" warnings or apps refusing to update
- Apps closing on their own or reloading when you switch between them
- Slow or unreliable Wi-Fi, and an easy path for a cable to the TV
- A desire to use it as part of a smart home with Matter or Thread devices

## Who should keep their Chromecast

Stick with what you have if:

- You use just a few apps, and they run well
- Your Wi-Fi is strong, or you already use an Ethernet adapter
- You have the 4K model and no storage problems
- It's on a secondary TV that doesn't get heavy use

The older HD model has more limits, particularly on a 4K TV. If you own that version and watch on a 4K set, an upgrade will make a more visible difference.

## Before you buy, free up space

Try these first, as they may solve your problem at no cost:

1. **Uninstall apps** you don't use.
2. **Clear app caches** under **Settings**, then **Apps**.
3. **Restart the device** to clear its memory.
4. **Move closer to Wi-Fi,** or use the 5 GHz band.
5. **Add a USB-C hub with Ethernet** if your Wi-Fi is the real cause.

## Are there alternatives?

Consider these other options before you settle on the Streamer:

- **Low-cost Google TV boxes,** such as Walmart's onn. models, can be a better value if you're not concerned about extra storage or smart home features.
- **Higher-end Android TV devices** such as the Nvidia Shield suit people who want better upscaling or a more powerful box.
- **Other platforms** may fit your devices better. Compare [Google TV with Fire TV](/compare/google-tv-vs-fire-tv), or see [Chromecast vs Roku](/compare/chromecast-vs-roku).

Avoid cheap uncertified boxes that claim similar specs. Our [Android TV box guide](/guides/android-tv-box) explains how to spot them.

## Our recommendation

If your Chromecast feels fine, keep it. If you're buying new, or if storage and Wi-Fi problems bother you every day, the Google TV Streamer is a worthwhile upgrade. Compare its price with low-cost Google TV boxes first, since not everyone needs the extra storage.
