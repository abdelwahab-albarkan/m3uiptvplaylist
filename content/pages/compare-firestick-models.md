## Fire Stick 4K vs 4K Max: The big split: Fire OS vs Vega OS
### Essential recommendations for Fire Stick 4K vs 4K Max


Fire OS is based on Android, which is why it can run so many apps. Vega OS is Amazon's newer Linux-based system. It's designed to be efficient on low-cost hardware, but it installs apps only from the Amazon Appstore, which has a smaller selection.

- **Choose Fire OS** (4K Plus or 4K Max) if you want TiviMate or another app that isn't in the Appstore.
- **Consider Vega OS** (HD or 4K Select) only if every app you need is available in the Appstore on that model.

Amazon has said future Fire TV Sticks will run Vega OS, so check the software on any new model before you buy. Our [Fire TV setup guide](/setup/firestick) explains what changes on each system.

## 4K Plus vs 4K Max

These are the two to compare if you want the most app choice. The 4K Max is the better option for heavy use:

- **Storage:** double the space, which helps if you keep many apps or a large channel guide.
- **Wi-Fi 6E:** useful only if your router also supports it, and most helpful in busy apartment buildings.
- **Long-term headroom:** extra storage and a faster connection can keep it feeling responsive for longer.

The 4K Plus is enough for many homes, especially with only a few apps installed and a Wi-Fi 5 or Wi-Fi 6 router. If you're choosing purely on price, compare the two on the day you're buying, because Amazon's sales can shrink the gap.

## HD vs 4K: is the upgrade worth it?

For a 4K TV, yes. Beyond resolution, the 4K models add HDR formats and support for higher-quality streams. If your main TV may be replaced soon, buying a 4K stick now also saves you buying another later.

For a small kitchen or guest room TV with basic apps, the HD model can be enough, provided that you've checked that your apps are available for it.

## What about the Fire TV Cube?

The Cube is a small box rather than a stick. It adds hands-free Alexa and generally has stronger hardware than the sticks. It's worth considering if you want a faster experience, or if you prefer a device that sits in the open rather than behind the TV. Our [Cube vs Stick comparison](/compare/fire-tv-cube-vs-stick) has the details.

## Three things to check before you buy

1. **Your apps.** Search for each must-have app and confirm it works on the exact model.
2. **Your connection.** A Wi-Fi 6E stick won't help with a weak signal or an old router. For a wired connection, look at Amazon's Ethernet adapter and confirm that it's compatible.
3. **Your TV's ports.** For 4K HDR, plug the stick into an HDMI port that supports HDCP 2.2, and use the extender if space behind the TV is tight.

## Our recommendation

For most people who want maximum app choice, the 4K Plus is the sensible pick, and the 4K Max is worth paying more for if you want extra storage and better Wi-Fi. Buy a Vega OS model only after confirming that every app you need is available.
