## Chromecast vs Roku: How each one works

### Essential recommendations for Chromecast vs Roku

**Classic Chromecast** has no home screen of its own. You open an app on your phone or computer, tap the Cast icon, and the TV plays the video. Your phone acts as the remote.

**Chromecast with Google TV**, the later version, added a remote and a full home screen. It works more like a Roku, but it can also receive Google Cast from your phone.

**Roku** has always worked as a standalone device. You open apps on the TV and control them with the remote, without needing a phone.

## What's changed for buyers

Google stopped making Chromecast devices and now sells the **Google TV Streamer** instead. You might still find leftover Chromecast units on sale, but before buying one, consider how long it will remain useful. For the differences between the two, see our [Google TV Streamer vs Chromecast guide](/compare/google-tv-streamer-vs-chromecast).

## Which is easier to use?

**For non-technical family members:** Roku wins. Its menus are predictable, and apps stay where you put them.

**For people who live on their phone:** Google TV wins. You can search or queue video on your phone, tap Cast, and pause from the notification on your phone. That's useful for showing a clip or letting a visitor share a video without logging in to the TV.

## Apps and content

Mainstream services run on both. The bigger differences show up beyond the most popular apps:

- **Roku** has its own free channels and a straightforward store, but it doesn't allow outside apps. That limits IPTV players, as explained in our guide to [IPTV on Roku](/setup/roku).
- **Google TV** uses Google Play, plus sideloading on most devices. That supports players such as [TiviMate](/apps/tivimate).

## Picture and sound

At comparable prices, both platforms offer 4K HDR models. Roku's lineup has a wide range of entry-level players, and our [Roku model comparison](/compare/roku-models) can help you choose. On Google TV, the options range from low-cost boxes to the Nvidia Shield. In both cases, look for Dolby Vision and Atmos support if your TV and speakers can use them.

## What about Fire TV and Apple TV?

- **Fire TV** has strong Alexa integration and low sale prices, but its home screen is heavy with promotions. See [Google TV vs Fire TV](/compare/google-tv-vs-fire-tv).
- **Apple TV 4K** costs more, but it's fast and works well with iPhones through AirPlay. See [Apple TV vs Fire Stick](/compare/apple-tv-vs-fire-stick).

## Our recommendation

Today's real choice is Roku or a Google TV device. Choose Roku if you want something simple that anyone can use with a remote. Choose Google TV if you cast from your phone, want more apps, or use Android. Only buy a leftover Chromecast if it's very cheap and you understand it's a discontinued product.
