## HD Homerun: What an HDHomeRun does

### Essential recommendations for HD Homerun

An HDHomeRun, made by SiliconDust, is a TV tuner that plugs into your router instead of your TV. Here's how a typical setup works:

**Antenna → HDHomeRun → router → your TVs, phones and computers**

This has one big benefit: you can put the antenna and tuner wherever reception is best, such as by an upstairs window, rather than next to your main TV. Any compatible device on your network can then watch.

It doesn't include any channels or a subscription to live TV. You get whatever free broadcast stations your antenna can receive.

## What you'll need

- A TV antenna and a coaxial cable
- An HDHomeRun tuner
- A router with a free Ethernet port (many models connect by wire)
- A compatible device with the HDHomeRun app or other supported software

## How to set it up

1. **Check your reception first.** Enter your address into the FCC's DTV Reception Maps, and note which direction the towers are in.
2. **Place the antenna** high up and near a window that faces those towers.
3. **Connect it.** Run the coaxial cable from the antenna to the HDHomeRun, and an Ethernet cable from the HDHomeRun to your router, then plug in the power.
4. **Install the app** on your streaming device, phone or computer.
5. **Scan for channels.** Follow the app's prompts, and keep a note of which stations come in reliably.
6. **Test each TV,** especially those on Wi-Fi, since they'll depend on your network for smooth playback.

If you lose channels, move the antenna before you buy anything else. A small change in position can make a big difference.

## Recording shows

To record, you'll need DVR software and somewhere to store your recordings. Options include HDHomeRun's own DVR service, which has required a paid subscription, along with its storage options. Third-party software such as Plex or Channels may also work, though these can have fees of their own. Check the current requirements before you buy, because pricing and features change.

If you'd prefer one simple box with fewer decisions, read our [Tablo vs HDHomeRun comparison](/compare/tablo-vs-hdhomerun).

## Choosing a model

Consider these factors when you compare models:

- **Number of tuners.** Count how many channels you'll want to watch or record at once.
- **ATSC 3.0 (NextGen TV).** Some models, such as the HDHomeRun Flex 4K, support this newer broadcast standard. Check whether stations in your area use it. Some ATSC 3.0 broadcasts use encryption, which can limit playback, so read current compatibility details.
- **Connection.** Check that your router has a spare port, or add a small network switch.
- **Your streaming devices.** Make sure the app is available and works well on what you own.

## Common problems

| Problem | What to try |
| --- | --- |
| App doesn't find the tuner | Check that the device and tuner are on the same network, then restart both |
| Few or no channels | Move the antenna higher or nearer a window, then rescan |
| Picture breaks up | Check the signal in the app, and adjust the antenna |
| Stutter on one TV only | That TV's Wi-Fi is likely the problem; try 5 GHz or Ethernet |
| "No tuners available" | All tuners are in use, so stop a stream or recording |

## Is it worth it?

An HDHomeRun makes sense if you get good antenna reception and want local channels on several devices, or if you enjoy customizing your setup. If you only need local channels on one TV, plugging the antenna straight into that TV costs less and is simpler.
