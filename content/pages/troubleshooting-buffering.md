## IPTV Buffering: First, find the bottleneck
### Essential recommendations for IPTV Buffering


Run a speed test **on the device that buffers**. On Fire TV and Android TV, install a speed test app from the store, or open fast.com in the device's browser. Compare it with this table:

| Stream type | Steady speed needed per screen |
| --- | --- |
| SD | 3–5 Mbps |
| HD (720p/1080p) | 8–10 Mbps |
| 4K | 25 Mbps |

- **Speed test below the target?** Your home network or connection is the issue. Start at fix 1.
- **Speed test well above the target, but it still buffers?** The device or the stream is more likely. Skip to fix 6.

## Network fixes

**1. Use Ethernet.** A cable from router to TV removes Wi-Fi interference completely. If running a cable isn't practical, a pair of powerline or MoCA adapters is the next best thing.

**2. Move to 5 GHz Wi-Fi.** The 2.4 GHz band is slower and crowded by neighbors, microwaves and Bluetooth. Most routers show two network names, or one name with *band steering*. Make sure the TV joins the 5 GHz band.

**3. Improve the signal path.** Wi-Fi struggles through brick, mirrors, aquariums and metal TV mounts. Moving the router a few feet higher, or adding a mesh node near the TV, can double the real speed. For sticks behind a wall-mounted TV, use the HDMI extender so the stick isn't sitting against metal.

**4. Pause heavy users.** Game downloads, cloud backups and 4K video calls on other devices all compete with your stream. Many routers have a *QoS* setting that gives streaming devices priority.

**5. Restart the router and change DNS.** A router that's been up for months can slow down. While you're in its settings, try switching DNS to a public resolver such as Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Google (8.8.8.8). That speeds up how quickly streams start, though it won't change steady speed.

## Device fixes

**6. Restart and clear space.** Fully power-cycle the device. On Fire TV and Android TV, clear the player's cache under Settings, then Applications, and uninstall apps you don't use. Devices with less than about 500 MB free tend to stutter.

**7. Match frame rate.** If video "jerks" rather than pausing, that's judder, not buffering. Turn on frame-rate matching in the device settings and in the player.

**8. Try the other decoder.** In the player's settings, switch between hardware and software decoding. Older sticks struggle with some HEVC 4K streams in software mode.

## Player and stream fixes

**9. Increase the buffer.** TiviMate, XCIPTV and others let you raise the buffer size. A larger buffer starts a second or two later but rides out short drops.

**10. Switch the stream format.** Some players let you choose *MPEG-TS* or *HLS* output. If one stutters, try the other.

**11. Pick the right quality.** If your speed test is marginal, watch the HD version of a channel instead of 4K. A smooth 1080p picture looks better than a 4K picture that stops every minute.

**12. Check whether it's your provider's path.** If everything at home tests fine but some channels still buffer in the evening, your internet provider may be congested on the route to the server. Our [VPN and throttling guide](/troubleshooting/vpn-for-iptv) explains how to test this safely.

## Still buffering?

Note the channel, time and device, run a speed test on the TV, and send those details to support. With that information, it's much easier to tell whether the problem is one stream, one server, or your connection.
