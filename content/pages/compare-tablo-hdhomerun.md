## Tablo vs Hdhomerun: The short answer

### Essential recommendations for Tablo vs Hdhomerun

- **Choose Tablo** if you want a simple antenna DVR that your whole household can use, with few decisions to make.
- **Choose HDHomeRun** if you want a flexible tuner that works with software such as Plex or Channels, or you plan to build a more advanced setup.

Before you buy either, check the FCC's DTV Reception Maps. Neither box can fix an address that doesn't receive a usable signal.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Tablo | HDHomeRun |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | All-in-one antenna DVR | Network TV tuner |
| Best for | Simple household use | Flexible, customizable setups |
| Recording | Built around DVR use | Needs DVR software and storage |
| Guide and fees | Vary by model and plan | Extended guide and DVR have required a subscription |

| ATSC 3.0 | Check the specific model | Available on models such as the Flex 4K |

Both companies change their products and plans over time, so confirm current hardware, fees and app support before you order.

## Ease of use

Tablo aims to work as a complete product: connect the antenna, install the app, and browse your guide. That usually makes it easier for family members who just want to watch and record shows.

HDHomeRun can also be simple if you only want live TV in its own app. Its real strength shows up when you combine it with other software, which takes more time to configure and adds more things that can go wrong.

## Recording

This is where the two differ the most, so think about it before anything else:

- **How much do you want to record?** Check the included storage and whether you can add more.
- **Where will recordings live?** HDHomeRun setups can use different storage options, including network storage, depending on the DVR software and hardware you choose.
- **What will it cost over time?** Add up any yearly fee, storage and software, rather than comparing box prices alone.

A cheap tuner can turn out more expensive than an all-in-one box after a few years of fees and extra hardware, and the reverse can be true as well.

## Picture and tuners

Picture quality depends largely on your signal strength, the original broadcast and your network. Compare these practical details instead:

- **Number of tuners:** this limits how many channels you can watch or record at once.
- **Commercial skip:** availability and quality vary, so check for your model and market.
- **NextGen TV:** ATSC 3.0 is growing but isn't available everywhere, and some encrypted broadcasts have compatibility limits.

## Network and placement

Both boxes let you put the antenna where reception is strongest. Keep these points in mind:

- Run Ethernet to the box where possible, especially for several streams.
- Test Wi-Fi on the TV farthest from your router.
- Check that the app runs on all your devices. Our [Roku](/setup/roku) and [Fire TV](/setup/firestick) guides explain how those platforms differ, including their app limits.

## Which one should you buy?

Pick Tablo if a simple, family-friendly DVR is what you're after. Pick HDHomeRun if you already use Plex or similar software, or you want more control and don't mind more setup. If you only want local channels on a single TV and don't need recording, you may not need either one. A good antenna connected directly to the TV could be all you need.
