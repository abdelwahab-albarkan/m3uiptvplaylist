## Roku vs Firestick: The short answer
### Essential recommendations for Roku vs Firestick


- **Choose Roku** if you want the simplest possible experience for mainstream streaming apps, a neutral home screen, and AirPlay from an iPhone.
- **Choose Fire Stick** if you use Alexa, want the widest app selection, or plan to use IPTV players like TiviMate, which Roku can't run.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Roku | Fire TV Stick |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home screen | Simple app grid; some ads in the sidebar | Content-heavy rows with Amazon promotions |
| App store | Roku Channel Store | Amazon Appstore, plus sideloading on Fire OS models |
| IPTV players | Limited, changes often | Nearly all Android players (TiviMate, Smarters, etc.) |
| Voice assistant | Roku Voice; works with Alexa and Google speakers | Alexa built in |
| AirPlay | Yes, on most 4K models | No |
| Google Cast | No | No (use the app instead) |
| HDR formats | HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision (4K models) | HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision (4K models) |
| Free live channels | The Roku Channel | Amazon's free live channels |
| Ethernet | Roku Ultra has a port | Adapter accessory for most sticks |

Specific features vary by model and change with updates, so check the current model page before you buy.

## Ease of use

Roku's biggest strength is consistency. Apps sit in a grid you arrange once, and the interface barely changes between models or years. Fire TV puts Amazon content front and center, with autoplaying previews and rows of recommendations. Some people like the discovery; others find it cluttered.

## Ads and privacy

Both platforms are ad-supported. Roku shows a banner ad on the home screen and promotes its own channel. Fire TV shows more prominent promotions and sometimes full-screen ads. Both let you limit ad tracking in the privacy settings, and it's worth doing on either one.

## Apps and IPTV

Every major streaming app is on both platforms. The difference is everything else:

- **Fire TV models running Fire OS** can install Android apps from outside the Amazon Appstore, which opens up TiviMate, IPTV Smarters Pro, XCIPTV, Kodi and more. Our [Fire Stick IPTV guide](/setup/firestick) shows how. The Fire TV Stick 4K Select, the 2026 HD and future Fire TV Sticks run Amazon's Vega OS and can't sideload. Our [Fire TV Stick comparison](/compare/firestick-models) lists the differences.
- **Roku** only runs apps from its own store. IPTV options are thin, so many Roku owners add a cheap stick for IPTV. We cover workarounds in [IPTV on Roku](/setup/roku).

## Picture and sound

At the 4K level, both deliver excellent picture with Dolby Vision and HDR10+. Fire TV's 4K Max models add Wi-Fi 6E, useful on newer routers. Roku's Ultra includes Ethernet and a remote finder. For Dolby Atmos, both pass it through to compatible soundbars on their 4K models.

## What about Google TV?

If neither feels right, Google TV devices run the same Android apps as Fire TV, including TiviMate and other IPTV players, with a cleaner interface and Google Cast built in. See our [Android TV and Google TV guide](/setup/android-tv).

## Our recommendation

For most households that just stream Netflix and similar apps, either is fine, so buy whichever matches your smart speaker or phone. If IPTV matters to you, a Fire TV Stick 4K Plus or 4K Max (or a Google TV device) is the clear choice. If you already own a Roku TV, there's no need to replace it. Plug one of those into a spare HDMI port and use each for what it does best.
