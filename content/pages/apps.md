## How to choose

**Start with your device.** If you watch on a Fire TV or Google TV, TiviMate is the default recommendation for most people because its guide grid and channel switching feel closest to cable. On Samsung or LG TVs, you're limited to what their stores offer, and IBO Player is the common choice. On Apple devices, the paid tvOS players are the most polished.

**Then think about how you watch.** If you mainly channel-surf live TV, prioritize the guide (EPG) and fast zapping. If you mostly watch movies and series on demand, choose a player that shows posters, descriptions and resume points cleanly. That favors Smarters and Apple's players.

**Consider the household.** Players that support multiple profiles or parental PINs, such as TiviMate Premium, help when kids use the same TV.

## Free or paid?

Free players cover the basics well: logging in, browsing channels and playing streams. Paid options usually add a more polished guide, recording, catch-up, profiles or multiple playlists. Try a free player first. If you find yourself wanting a faster guide or better organization on a TV you use every day, a small fee can be worthwhile. Check the current price inside the app or official store, and remember that the fee covers the software only, not channels.

## Can I use a web player?

Browser-based players need no install, but they support fewer stream types and may see your login. Read [our web player guide](/apps/iptv-web-player) before you enter your details on one.

## Xtream Codes or M3U?

Most modern players accept both login types. Xtream Codes is usually easier because you enter a server, username and password, and the player can sort live TV, movies and series for you. An M3U link is more universal, and it's what simple players such as VLC need. Our [M3U and Xtream Codes guide](/guides/m3u-playlist) explains the difference.

## Three things to avoid

1. **Players bundled with channels.** A legitimate player never includes content. If an app comes "preloaded," it's distributing streams it almost certainly has no rights to.
2. **Modded or "cracked" versions of paid apps.** They're a common source of malware on TV boxes and stop updating.
3. **Fake activation websites.** For MAC-activated players, only enter your details on the developer's real site, which is linked from inside the app.
