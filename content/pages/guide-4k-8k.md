## 4K IPTV Service: What "4K" actually means
### Essential recommendations for 4K IPTV Service


4K (Ultra HD) is 3840 × 2160 pixels, four times as many as 1080p. In streaming, resolution is only part of the story:

- **Bitrate** is how much data per second the stream carries. A 4K stream with a low bitrate can look worse than a good 1080p stream.
- **Codec** is how efficiently the video is compressed. Most 4K streams use **HEVC (H.265)**. Newer ones may use **AV1**, which is even more efficient.
- **HDR** (High Dynamic Range) adds brighter highlights and richer color. Formats include HDR10, HLG (common for live broadcasts) and Dolby Vision.
- **Frame rate** matters for sports. 4K at 60 fps needs more data than 4K at 30 fps.

## What you need for real 4K

| Requirement | Recommended |
| --- | --- |
| Internet | A steady 25 Mbps per 4K stream, with Ethernet or strong 5 GHz Wi-Fi |
| Device | 4K-capable streamer with HEVC hardware decoding: Fire TV Stick 4K Plus / 4K Max, Google TV Streamer, Chromecast with Google TV 4K, Apple TV 4K, Nvidia Shield |
| TV | 4K TV; HDR support for HDR channels |
| HDMI | Device plugged into an HDMI port that supports 4K at 60 Hz with HDCP 2.2 (check the TV's port labels) |
| Player | An app that supports HEVC and shows stream info, such as TiviMate |

The HD Chromecast, the basic Fire TV Stick and older Roku Express models can't output 4K.

## How to check if a channel is really 4K

Many "4K" channels are 1080p upscaled. To check:

1. **Look at stream info.** In TiviMate, press OK and open the stream info panel. Many other players have an info or "i" button. You want **3840×2160** and a codec of **HEVC** or **AV1**.
2. **Check your TV's info banner.** Most TVs show the incoming signal, but this only tells you what the *device* is sending, which may be upscaled.
3. **Look at fine detail.** True 4K sports show individual blades of grass and readable crowd signs. Upscaled 1080p looks soft by comparison.

## The truth about 8K IPTV

8K is 7680 × 4320 pixels. The reality in the US today:

- **Almost no TV is produced in 8K.** Networks and leagues broadcast in HD, with select events in 4K.
- **8K streams need roughly 50 to 100 Mbps** and a device that can decode them. Very few streaming devices can.
- **"8K" in a product or service name usually describes the TV it supports or the brand, not the content.**

If you own an 8K TV, it will upscale 4K and HD content, and often does that very well. Just don't pay extra for an "8K stream" without checking what's actually delivered.

## Getting the most from 4K

- Turn on **Match Dynamic Range** and **Match Frame Rate** on your streaming device.
- Use the TV's **Filmmaker** or **Movie** mode for HDR content rather than Vivid.
- If 4K buffers but HD doesn't, your connection is the limit. See the [buffering guide](/troubleshooting/iptv-buffering).
