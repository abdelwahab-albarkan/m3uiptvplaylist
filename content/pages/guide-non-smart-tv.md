## Amazon Fire Stick Non Smart TV: What you need

### Essential recommendations for Amazon Fire Stick Non Smart TV

A streaming stick does the smart TV's job, so the TV only has to display the picture. You'll need:

1. **An HDMI port** on the TV
2. **A streaming stick,** such as a Fire TV Stick, Roku stick or Google TV device
3. **Wi-Fi** at home with a usable speed
4. **A power outlet.** Plugging into the wall works better than a TV's USB port, which may not provide enough power
5. **An account** for the stick, such as Amazon or Google, and for any apps that require one

Your TV doesn't need its own internet connection or any smart features.

## How to check your TV's ports

Look on the back and side of your TV for a narrow port labeled **HDMI**. Most flat-panel TVs from the last 15 years or so have at least one. If there's only one and something else already uses it, a small HDMI switch lets you share it between devices.

## Step-by-step setup

1. **Plug the stick into HDMI.** If it doesn't fit, use the short extender that often comes in the box.
2. **Connect the power adapter** to a wall outlet.
3. **Turn on the TV** and choose the matching HDMI input with the **Input** or **Source** button on your TV remote.
4. **Pair the stick's remote,** following the on-screen instructions.
5. **Connect to Wi-Fi** and enter your password.
6. **Sign in** and install updates. The first update can take several minutes.
7. **Install your apps** and test a video.

Our [Fire TV guide](/setup/firestick) explains further options for that platform.

## If your TV has no HDMI

Very old TVs may only have red, white and yellow RCA inputs or a coaxial antenna connection. An **HDMI-to-RCA converter** can sometimes connect a stick, but expect these limits:

- **Lower picture quality.** The picture is reduced to standard definition, so small text may be hard to read.
- **HDCP problems.** Streaming devices use copy protection, and some converters don't support it. You may see a blank screen or error message.
- **More to power.** Converters usually need their own USB power.
- **Reliability varies.** Some combinations won't work, so buy from somewhere that accepts returns.

Before spending much on a converter, compare the cost with a basic new TV. Even a small HDTV may be a better option.

## Which stick should you buy?

- **For a 1080p TV:** a basic model is usually fine, but check that it supports the apps you need. Fire TV's newer Vega OS sticks have fewer apps. See our [Fire TV Stick comparison](/compare/firestick-models).
- **For very simple use:** Roku is easy to learn. See our [Roku model comparison](/compare/roku-models).
- **If you may buy a 4K TV soon:** a 4K stick also works on a 1080p TV, so you can reuse it later.

## Common problems and fixes

| Problem | What to try |
| --- | --- |
| "No signal" | Check the TV's input, reseat the stick, and try another HDMI port |
| Blank screen with HDCP error | Try a different HDMI port, and remove any cheap splitters or converters |
| Remote won't respond | Replace the batteries and repeat pairing |
| Stick keeps restarting | Use the original power adapter in a wall outlet |
| Picture doesn't fit | Check the display settings on the stick and the zoom or aspect setting on the TV |
| Sound is missing | Change the audio setting from surround to stereo |

## Getting good performance from an old TV

Older TVs often have slower HDMI processing, and their speakers can be weak, but the stick will handle streaming on its own. If video buffers, the cause is usually Wi-Fi rather than the TV. Our [buffering guide](/troubleshooting/iptv-buffering) has fixes, and many also apply to services like Netflix and YouTube. For better sound, connect a soundbar or speakers using your TV's audio output, if it has one.


When choosing a reliable **Amazon Fire Stick Non Smart TV**, verifying network peering and server routing ensures optimal playback stability.
