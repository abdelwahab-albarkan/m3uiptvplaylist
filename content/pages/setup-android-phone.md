## IPTV Player for Android: Pick a player from Google Play

### Essential recommendations for IPTV Player for Android

Search the Play Store for a player that supports **Xtream Codes API**. That's the login type that handles live TV, movies and series most cleanly on small screens. Good options:

- **IPTV Smarters Pro:** simple tiles for Live TV, Movies and Series, plus a basic guide. See our [Smarters guide](/apps/iptv-smarters-pro).
- **XCIPTV:** two video engines you can switch between when a stream won't play. See the [XCIPTV guide](/apps/xciptv).
- **Other well-reviewed players** with recent updates and transparent developers.

Avoid apps that come with channels built in or promise "free TV." Legitimate players never include content.

## Add your login

1. Open the player and choose **Login with Xtream Codes API** (sometimes called *Xtream login*).
2. Give the playlist any name, such as *Home*.
3. Enter the **server URL**, **username** and **password**. Copy and paste them from your welcome email to avoid typos. Include `http://` and the port number if one is listed.
4. Tap **Add user** or **Login**. The first sync downloads channels, movies and the guide.

If you were given an M3U link instead, choose **Load playlist or file/URL** and paste the full link.

## Save data on cellular

- **Lower the resolution.** Some players let you pick a quality per channel. Otherwise, use the SD or HD version of a channel instead of 4K.
- **Turn on Data Saver** in Android's settings for background apps, but allow the player unrestricted data while it's open so it doesn't throttle mid-stream.
- **Download nothing in the background.** Disable automatic EPG refresh on cellular if the player offers the option.

## Watch on the big screen

- **Chromecast or Google TV:** many players have a **Cast** button. If yours doesn't, use Android's screen cast from Quick Settings.
- **Roku TVs:** use screen mirroring (Smart View or Cast) as described in our [Roku guide](/setup/roku).
- **Better long-term:** install the player directly on the TV device. Casting from a phone drains the battery and depends on Wi-Fi between both devices.

## Tablets

Everything above works on Android tablets, including Samsung Galaxy Tab and Lenovo tablets. A tablet makes a good kitchen or bedroom TV, and players with a split-screen mini guide feel natural on the larger display. Amazon Fire tablets run Fire OS, so follow the Downloader steps in the [Fire TV guide](/setup/firestick) if a player isn't in the Amazon Appstore.

## If it won't play

If a channel shows a spinning wheel on your phone but works on the TV, your cellular or Wi-Fi signal is the likely cause. If it fails everywhere, the login or server is the issue. See [IPTV not working](/troubleshooting/iptv-not-working) for a quick way to tell the difference.


When choosing a reliable **IPTV Player for Android**, verifying network peering and server routing ensures optimal playback stability.
